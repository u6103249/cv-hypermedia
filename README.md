## 1. Project Analysis

### User Profile (User Persona)
* **Name:** Sarah Jenkins
* **Role:** Senior Technical Recruiter / Hiring Manager at a Software Engineering Firm.
* **Goals:** 
  * Quickly identify a candidate's background, core programming skills, and contact information within seconds.
  * Evaluate academic progression and practical coursework at the University of Girona (UdG).
  * Direct access to external professional profiles (GitHub and LinkedIn).
* **Pain Points:** 
  * Cluttered multi-page CVs with poor visual hierarchy.
  * Difficulty finding concrete technical capabilities and soft skills.
* **Expectations:** A clean, responsive single-page layout with clear visual structure, accessible contact links, and modern UI design.

---

### Information Architecture (220 words)
The information architecture of this single-page CV website is organized into a structured two-column layout designed for maximum legibility and efficient scanning by technical recruiters.

The left column functions as a persistent personal sidebar containing primary identification elements: a professional profile photograph, direct contact channels (email and phone number), external social links (interactive GitHub and LinkedIn buttons), language proficiencies, and personal soft skills. Placing contact options and core personal traits in the sidebar ensures that recruiters can immediately initiate contact or assess soft skills without scrolling.

The main right column hosts the chronological and analytical content of the resume. It opens with a prominent header displaying my full name and current professional title (*Computer Engineering Student*), alongside a call-to-action button to download the PDF CV. Below the header, the 'About Me' section presents a concise executive overview of my academic profile at the University of Girona (UdG).

The rest of the content is divided into clearly defined cards: 'Technical Skills' (highlighting key technologies like C++, Python, Java, SQL, and Web Development), 'Education' (focusing on my current Bachelor's Degree in Computer Engineering and relevant coursework), and 'Experience & Projects' (emphasizing leadership roles, tutorship, and academic software developments). This modular arrangement allows readers to effortlessly jump directly to the section most relevant to their assessment needs.

### Visual Design (215 words)
The visual design proposal adopts a modern, clean, and tech-oriented aesthetic suitable for a computer engineering profile. The color scheme relies on a high-contrast palette: a deep slate blue (`#0F172A`) for sidebars and primary structural components, paired with a soft background (`#F8FAFC`) and crisp white card containers (`#FFFFFF`) to ensure superior readability. Vibrant indigo (`#6366F1`) and emerald green accents are strategically applied to highlight key section titles, button borders, and skill tags.

Typography utilizes clean sans-serif fonts (such as Inter) to deliver sharp legibility across digital displays. Clear typographic hierarchy is maintained by applying contrasting font weights and sizes: bold uppercase lettering for primary headers, semibold titles for institutions and job roles, and regular weight for body text.

Structural UI components rely on rounded card containers (12px to 16px corner radius) with subtle stroke borders. Interactive micro-interactions are integrated into actionable components—such as GitHub/LinkedIn buttons and skill pills—using subtle scale and border hover effects (`While Hovering`) to enhance user engagement without causing visual distraction. Overall, the visual design strikes a balance between professional engineering rigor and modern digital UI standards.
